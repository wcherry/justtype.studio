# Terms of use

## Your work is yours

Everything you write here — scenes, drafts, snapshots, notes, the whole book —
belongs to you. Using Scriptorium does not transfer, license or share your
copyright with us, and nothing in these terms changes that.

We store and display your work only to run the service for you: to save it,
to show it to the people you choose to show it to, and to give it back to you.
We do not publish it, sell it, train anything on it, or show it to anyone you
have not shared it with — with the one exception you can choose below.

## Summaries

Summaries of a scene, a chapter or a book are written by a model that
Anthropic runs, not by us. They are off until you turn them on in your
account. Once they are on, the text you ask to summarise — and only that
text, only when you ask — is sent to Anthropic to write the summary, and the
summary comes back to you. It is a suggestion: nothing in your manuscript
changes unless you put it there. You can turn summaries off at any time;
summaries already written stay with your work. How Anthropic handles what is
sent to it is set out in its own terms for its API.

## Sharing is your decision

A draft is private until you publish a version and offer it for review. When
you do, you choose who can read it: named readers, signed-in members, anyone
with the link, or everyone. You can withdraw a publication at any time.
Co-authors you add can work on the book according to the role you give them,
and you can take them off again.

## What readers leave

Notes, reactions and answers you leave on someone else's work belong to you as
writing, and the author is free to use them to revise their book. Be honest
and be kind: you can disagree with a book without attacking the person who
wrote it. An author can block a reader from their own work, and we may remove
an account that is used to harass people.

## Leaving

You can download everything you have here at any time, as files you can open
without us. You can delete your account at any time. Deleting it removes your
account, the works you started and their files. Notes you left on other
people's books are blanked rather than torn out, so the replies under them
still make sense; they no longer carry your name or your words.

## Security

You are responsible for keeping your password to yourself. Turn on two-factor
sign-in if your work matters to you. If you think someone else has got in,
reset your password — that signs out every device — and tell us.

## Changes to these terms

If these terms change in a way that matters, we will ask you to agree again
before you carry on. The version you agreed to, and when, is kept with your
account.
